# TILT Joker Lab · Real-player test sheet

**Hypothesis:** a full-pile Joker creates a memorable social climax. A visible-number counter lets players feel agency rather than merely suffer a random attack.

**Not a sales forecast.** Use this sheet to compare gameplay feelings, not to validate production.

## Physical setup, for two players

- Shuffle the 32 CORE cards with **two Joker faces**, remove 16 EXTRA cards.
- Deal four face-down-hand cards to each player and place the remaining 24 in a draw stock.
- Use identical opaque-backed sleeves. Put Joker cards *sideways* beside the pile, leaving the target number and pile size visible.
- Standard pickup is **one top card, rest discarded**. A **Joker** is different: full pile or valid counter. Refill to four only after playing while the draw stock remains.
- Use the short rules printed with the prototype. Test ONLY one rule mode per match, not a mix.
- Never coach people about the "right" Joker timing until after the session.

## Comparison

For each new two-person group, record 2–3 matches and rotate the mode order:

- **Plain:** Joker forces the victim to take the whole pile, no counter.
- **Joker-vs-Joker:** another Joker may reflect the whole pile.
- **Match Counter:** a normal card with the exact visible top number may reflect the attack.

Counterbalance order between groups (ABC, BCA, CAB, CBA, ACB, BAC as capacity allows). Randomize the starting player, not always the person hosting the phone.

If you have time for a fourth game, let them choose freely: play one more Joker variant, original ONE, Shithead, or stop. Record their actual choice without pressuring them to be nice.

## Fast follow-up interview

1. "When did you decide to save or use the Joker? What made you decide?"
2. "Before the attack, did you do anything to prepare or protect yourself?"
3. "What was your best decision that wasn't simply luck?"
4. "If you could remove one rule, what would it be?"
5. "Which version would you choose right now, for another game?"

Ask the two players separately before they discuss their answers.

## Failure signals

- Nobody cares about pile size until the Joker has already appeared.
- Keeping a Joker is always correct, with no interesting reason to play it earlier.
- A matching-number counter feels lucky, never earned.
- Full-pile pickups cause long, unfun recovery stretches.
- Attack on an empty pile functions as an annoying skip.
- Two Jokers cause too much downtime in one-on-one games.
- People can't explain the rank-counter rule despite the previous number being visibly displayed.
- People consistently prefer familiar Shithead or the earlier ONE prototype.

If any of these show up repeatedly, simplify or redesign the attack/counter, **not** by automatically adding more special numbers or using a best-of-three match wrapper.

## Quality gate before physical sale

At least **12 independent groups** who did not design the game, usable print proofs, real device QA, repeatable replay preference and clear evidence that normal turns offer meaningful choices. Complete a separate prior-art/IP review, manufacturing-cost study and product identity exercise before store launch.
