# SPARK Exchange: human playtest card (print or copy)

**Status:** exploratory. Do not tell players which ruleset we believe is better.

Use the self-contained [offline playtest](playtest.html). A group should compare the **Insert 9/3/3 control** and **Exchange 9/3/4** before trying **Exchange 7/3/4**. Rotate which is first between groups. Each player must see the rules themselves before playing. No hints from the developer once the round starts.

## After EACH ruleset (players answer independently, without group discussion)

| Question | Answer |
| --- | --- |
| Was your move usually obvious, or were you torn between alternatives? | 1 obvious – 5 genuinely torn |
| Did you feel you could influence the outcome? | 1 not at all – 5 strongly |
| Could you plan a move *before your next turn*? | 1 never – 5 often |
| Were your opponent's moves meaningful to you? | 1 not really – 5 very much |
| Were combo chains satisfying? | 1 not at all – 5 very |
| Would you voluntarily play again right now? | **Yes / No** |
| Would you teach this to a friend? | **Yes / No** |

**Open-ended, ask aloud without leading:**

- Which two moves did you seriously consider, and **what did you give up** by choosing one?
- What did you expect your opponent to do next?
- Describe one surprise, one repetitive moment, and one confusing rule.
- Did you want to play again because of an interesting decision, a close result, or just because the session was short?

## After both rulesets

- Which would you choose for another game **right now**? Insert / Exchange / No preference.
- What is your reason, in your own words?
- Would you pay **€16.90 / €19.90 / neither** for a physical boxed edition, knowing these are prototype rules? This is stated intent, **not proof of actual demand**.
- Did one ruleset benefit from being played second? Learning / group fatigue / no visible effect.

## Researcher scoring notes

- Keep the unit of analysis at **group and player** level; report denominators and trial order.
- Distinguish spontaneous second games from prompted ones.
- Record actual play time, not estimated simulation time.
- Do not interpret a higher combo or average score rate as a positive fun result.
- Track no-score turns where the player intentionally prepared a threat that paid off later.
- Log obvious repetitive cycles even if the complete row state is not literally repeated.
- Do not merge or deploy Exchange into the existing Sites game until unassisted playtests support it.

Suggested exploratory gate: 12 independent groups of varied experience. Do not treat 12 as a statistical power guarantee. Prefer a clearly stated, repeatable preference over a marginal rating difference.
