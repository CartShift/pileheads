# TILT / SPARK Next: Original Proprietary-Deck Card Game
**Status:** product-design brief v0.1, 2026-11-11. Working title TILT, **not cleared as a trademark**.
**Repository:** keep in this same spark-card-game repo; this is a new design exploration and does not supersede or alter existing playable products.

## Non-negotiable product decisions (from the owner)

1. **Inspiration, not a clone:** take the emotional appeal of the card game Shithead / Palace, but design a substantially different game with its own rules and identity.
2. **Reference house rules, confirmed by the owner on 2026-11-11:** **2 resets the play constraint** (the next player can start anew without burning the pile); **3 is transparent**, preserving the effective underlying card/rank; **7 requires the next played card to be strictly lower than 7**; **8 skips the next player**; **10 burns/clears the pile and grants the player an extra turn**. The 7 is a next-card restriction, not a permanent direction change. Some edge-case interactions, especially transparent 3 over 7 and combinations of special cards, remain unspecified. These are *reference rules*, not instructions to copy their effects into our proprietary game.
3. **Proprietary deck:** the retail game must be played with **purpose-designed, originally illustrated physical cards**, not a standard 52-card deck with a new back. The deck's content and information architecture should enable game rules that a conventional deck does not readily express.
4. **Own-store-first commercial path:** develop it as a consumer product for **direct sales through our own online store** (Shopify is a possible implementation, not a brand commitment). A conventional publisher-license pitch is not the default plan.
5. **Physical game first:** no app, server, tokens, smartphone, or electricity should be required to play. A digital implementation may facilitate rules testing and community play, not replace the physical product.
6. **Single focused product:** do not assume expansions, subscriptions, DLC, costly components, multiple tiers, special packaging or a Kickstarter until the core game passes human trials.

## What to preserve from Shithead

- A simple objective and intuitive "I want to get rid of my cards" urgency.
- A shared public danger that grows and can be redirected to other players.
- Big reversals, moments of rescue and occasional hilarious failure.
- Hand management, prepared visible cards and a tense finale.
- Cards whose abilities feel memorable and legible, not a text-heavy manual.
- Social interaction: a winning move changes the opponent's situation, rather than merely increasing one's own score.

## What we need to invent (not just rename)

- A **distinct strategic decision** per turn: choosing an option that is not obviously best because it changes the next player's choices.
- A **new, mechanically necessary deck attribute**, e.g. cards showing two independent values or one value plus a directional ability, rather than standard four suits and 2–A ranks.
- A distinctive consequence when a player cannot safely play, ideally an *interesting choice*, not just a mandatory pickup.
- Endgame decisions with some control over uncertain information, not pure blind punishment.
- A teachable turn structure. Five interesting powers in a game are useful only if a newcomer can understand them quickly.

### Important differentiation warning

Two ascending/descending piles alone are NOT a commercial differentiator: the existing card game *The Game* uses ascending and descending piles. Bespoke physical decks also already exist for Shithead; Shithead.io sells a customized deck with additional special cards. We must test the *full combination* of mechanics and its player experience, not claim novelty based on the packaging, two piles, or 5 special abilities.

## Card information design: prototype hypothesis (NOT a locked production specification)

An initial physical trial can use a **76-card custom deck**:
- **56 dual-purpose cards** each printed with two explicitly different gameplay values, such as a RISE and a FALL value. These are real separate parameters, not a single ordinary playing-card rank mirrored across the card. The two values create decisions about which shared lane a card helps or harms.
- **20 tactics cards**, 4 of each of five experimental function families. The reference to 2/3/7/8/10 tells us *which kinds of emotional beats people enjoy*, not that the new powers must be direct equivalents. Do not design a one-to-one renamed copy.

The 76-card count, per-type multiplicities, presence of 2 piles, and five tactic families are all **testable placeholders**, not commitments. We may reduce them, collapse one pile, or use an entirely different deck structure when testing reveals a better game. Any released deck must have balanced distribution for its intended player count and enough cards for the entire game without unprinted substitutes.

### Concepts to explore, without prematurely adopting a rule set

**Concept A, "linked danger":** two public piles with incompatible constraints. Playing a card to protect yourself in one lane also alters or threatens the other lane using information printed on the same card. Failed turns present a meaningful choice of which risk to take. Test against existing ascending/descending games for real differentiation.

**Concept B, "prepared escape":** a single shared danger track with unique dual-state card faces. A card can be played for immediate relief or kept as a visibly threatened/charged backup for the endgame. Test whether this genuinely beats a Shithead-like discard puzzle.

Both must preserve the fast, social, high-variance feel and avoid the overengineering that made the previous SPARK experiments frustrating.

## How to judge prototypes

Target (hypotheses, not independently validated specs):
- 2–5 players if feasible, start by proving 2 and 4.
- About 15–25 minutes; teach the core in 2–3 minutes.
- Players can name at least one real sacrifice, opponent trap, rescue, or deliberate reversal.
- Difficulty comes from consequences, not many special-card exceptions or hidden arithmetic.
- Better players have measurable opportunities to improve, but newer players can still win sometimes.
- Endgame tension rises; the final third should not collapse into powerless turns.

**Reference match:** compare against the owner's actual five-special-card Shithead variant, with the same friend group. If our prototype is not preferred for replay, do not assume it is worth retailing. Test physically first.

Minimum validation:
1. Treat all five reference effects as confirmed: **2 = reset**, **3 = transparent**, **7 = next card strictly below 7**, **8 = skip next player**, **10 = burn pile + extra turn**. Resolve only genuinely ambiguous interaction details (especially how a 3 interacts with a preceding 7) when building a faithful baseline. Do not require the new game to reproduce these powers one-to-one.
2. Develop two very small paper mockups, changing one core difference at a time; do not commit to art or production.
3. Simulate conservation, forced moves, first-seat advantage, distribution and likely stalled endings.
4. Playtest in person, including strangers to the design. Ask whether they'd immediately rematch and why.
5. Blind-playtest rulebook without designer assistance.
6. Investigate existing games / substantial overlap and conduct actual brand/name/trademark checks before launch.

## Product and own-store business model

The **physical deck in a branded box** is the primary SKU. Digital play, a rules site, and community content can help acquisition, but are not prerequisites to enjoying the physical product.

The first sales channel is our own storefront, initially focused on Germany/EU if fulfillment is viable; this is a working market assumption to validate, not a shipping promise. Plan packaging, shelf appeal, unboxing, card tactile quality, legible iconography, accessibility for color-vision differences, clear multi-language rules if needed, and barcode/retail readiness only when appropriate.

Before spending on manufacturing, compare:
- Prototype and print run/MOQ unit cost;
- Packaging and card stock/finish;
- VAT and other taxes as applicable;
- Payment processing, pick/pack, shipping subsidies, import and fulfillment;
- Return/defect reserve;
- Customer-acquisition cost and conversion evidence.

**Contribution per order = net collected revenue (excluding applicable VAT) - unit production/landed cost - payment fees - fulfillment & shipping costs borne by us - expected returns/defects - customer acquisition cost.** No assumed profitable price or guaranteed ROI.

### Decisions deliberately left open

- Reference house-rule edge cases (e.g., a transparent 3 over a 7 and special-card stacking). The basic effects of all five reference specials are confirmed;
- Final game name and visual world;
- Number and effects of unique cards;
- Exact deck size, number of players, modes, win condition and whether special cards are numbers or illustrated roles;
- Selling price and manufacturing partners.

**Guardrail:** Do not merge an existing Duel/CLAIM/SIGNAL ruleset into this retail brief just because working code exists. These prototypes can provide reusable testing infrastructure, but this new game's mechanics must earn their place through playtesting.
